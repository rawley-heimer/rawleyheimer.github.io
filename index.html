About

Professor Rawley Heimer is an expert in financial markets whose research examines how households build and lose financial security through their decisions in investment and credit markets. His research shows how social networks shape investor behavior, how leverage drives retail investors’ trading and affects financial markets, and how mistaken beliefs distort saving and investment over the life cycle. Current work examines how AI affects personal finances. He also studies real estate markets, including mortgage lending and institutional investment in housing.

His research is published in leading finance and economics journals and has received numerous awards, including the Dimensional Fund Advisors Distinguished Paper Award at the Journal of Finance and the Rising Scholar Award at the Review of Financial Studies. This research has been featured in Bloomberg News, the Financial Times, Forbes, and the Wall Street Journal, among others.

Prof. Heimer is an Associate Editor at Management Science, the Review of Corporate Financial Studies, and Financial Management. Outside of academia, he was formerly an economist at the Federal Reserve Bank and served as Senior Advisor for Research and Policy at the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau. He frequently gives presentations to asset managers and supports litigation in areas such as consumer banking, financial markets, and digital assets.

Research by topic

  • Retail investing and brokerage

    How retail investors trade, why they so often trade against their own interests, and how leverage, trading costs, brokerage practices, and social networks change their outcomes.

  • Social media and finance

    How online social networks and social media shape what retail investors believe and how they trade.

  • Consumer credit and debt

    How households get, use, and repay credit, and how early-life conditions, cash windfalls, regulation, and politics shape credit access and credit outcomes.

  • Local banking and access to finance

    How access to local banks and credit markets shapes the financial lives of households and small businesses over the long run.

  • Mortgage lending and housing

    Who gets mortgage credit and on what terms, how racial disparities in lending emerge, and how institutional investors affect local housing markets.

  • Consumer protection and financial regulation

    How regulation, supervision, and politics affect people who use financial services.

  • Saving, retirement, and inheritance

    How people accumulate, spend down, and pass on wealth over the life cycle.

  • Behavioral finance and beliefs

    Why people’s financial choices depart from what standard economic models predict.

  • Legal and regulatory institutions

    How courts, regulators, and politics shape credit markets and what consumers get from them.
