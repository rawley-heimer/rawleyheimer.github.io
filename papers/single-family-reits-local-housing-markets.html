Working paper

Single-Family REITs and Local Housing Markets

Marco Giacoletti, Rawley Z. Heimer, Wenli Li, and Edison Yu

Abstract. Institutional investors in single-family housing markets are often represented as worsening household well-being. We document the growth of single-family REITs (SFRs) and their (non-)effects on housing markets by constructing a novel dataset of SFRs’ underlying properties. SFRs purchase properties in neighborhoods encircling city centers, where housing supply is more elastic, and with populations facing homeownership barriers. Using a spatial differences empirical strategy, SFR growth relates to increased housing supply, lower mortgage approval rates, and modest price increases. These findings likely capture SFRs’ selection of neighborhoods rather than causal effects. SFR asset returns mirror representative housing portfolios despite their concentrated holdings.

Keywords: single-family rental, institutional investors, REITs, housing supply, house prices, homeownership, mortgage approvals.

In brief

Large institutional landlords in single-family housing are often blamed for hurting households, but this paper finds little evidence that they cause harm.

Building a new dataset of the homes owned by single-family REITs, the authors show these firms buy in neighborhoods ringing city centers, where housing supply is more elastic and residents face barriers to homeownership. REIT growth is associated with more housing supply, lower mortgage approval rates, and modest price increases, though these patterns likely reflect where REITs choose to buy rather than effects they cause. Returns on REIT housing portfolios mirror those of representative housing portfolios despite their concentrated holdings.

Contributes to research on institutional investors in housing, housing supply and prices, and homeownership.
