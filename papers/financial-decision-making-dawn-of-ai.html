Oxford Research Encyclopedia of Economics and Finance, forthcoming

Financial Decision Making, at the Dawn of AI

Rawley Z. Heimer

Abstract. This article gives perspective on the academic literature on personal financial decision making. I explore three main domains of financial choices: savings versus consumption, investment decision making, and choices around debt. In each domain, people behave in ways that deviate from normative prescriptions of financial economics. I explore the main drivers of financial decisions that result in these deviations. The article concludes with a forward-looking perspective of how generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) might reshape the landscape of individual’s financial decisions.

Keywords: household finance, personal financial decision making, generative AI, saving and consumption, investment mistakes, debt choices, behavioral finance survey.

In brief

A review of research on how people make personal financial decisions, and of how generative AI might change them.

A review of research on how people make personal financial decisions — saving versus spending, investing, and borrowing — and why their choices often depart from what financial economics prescribes. The article surveys the main drivers of those departures in each domain. It closes with a forward-looking discussion of how generative AI might reshape the way individuals make financial decisions.
