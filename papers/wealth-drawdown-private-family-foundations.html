The Wealth Draw-down of the Fabulously Rich: Insights from Private Family Foundations

Changhyun Ahn and Rawley Z. Heimer

Abstract. We connect genealogical records to the universe of 501(c)(3) private foundations to provide novel insights into the late-in-life assets holdings and charitable contributions of the wealthiest households in the U.S. This linkage allows us to track life-cycle financial assets and charitable disbursements for 8,276 families, each holding an average of $20.65 million in family foundation assets annually. In contrast to life-cycle models that predict a steady draw-down of assets late in life, we find that the death of the eldest family member leads to a 96.9% increase in foundation assets within two years. Yet few are prepared to distribute their wealth postmortem — charitable disbursements take years to match post-death inflows and funds are more likely to fall below the five-percent rule to qualify for non-profit status. We also find that the doubling of the lifetime estate and gift tax exemption in the 2017 Tax Cuts and Jobs Act caused a 47.2% reduction in postmortem inflows into family foundations, which suggests that many family foundations are used to reduce estate taxes. These findings contribute to our understanding of inter-generational wealth inequality.

Keywords: wealth decumulation, private family foundations, intergenerational wealth, bequest motives, charitable giving, estate taxation, Tax Cuts and Jobs Act, donor-advised funds.

JEL codes: D31, D64, G51, H24.

In brief

When the eldest member of a very wealthy family dies, the family’s private foundation roughly doubles in size within two years, the opposite of the gradual late-life drawdown that standard life-cycle models predict.

Linking IRS filings for the universe of U.S. private foundations to genealogical records, the paper tracks 8,276 family foundations holding an average of $20.65 million in assets. Charitable giving lags far behind the post-death inflows, which come mostly from surviving family trustees and often as noncash assets, and foundations become more likely to fall below the five-percent payout requirement. After the 2017 Tax Cuts and Jobs Act doubled the estate and gift tax exemption, post-death inflows fell by 47.2%, suggesting many families use foundations to manage estate taxes.

Contributes to research on wealth decumulation and late-life saving, bequests and charitable giving, estate taxation, and wealth at the top of the distribution.
