Economic Commentary 2016-13, Federal Reserve Bank of Cleveland

Geographic Mobility and Consumer Financial Health: Evidence from Oil Production Boom Towns

Rawley Z. Heimer, Timothy Stehulak, and Caitlin Treanor

Abstract. One way a household might handle financial distress is to relocate to another area that offers greater income opportunities. This article examines the impact of geographic mobility on consumer finances by focusing on the residents of “boom towns” — areas that saw a surge of growth in oil-drilling activity around 2010 and a bust thereafter. We find that residents who move after the bust experience stronger consumer financial health than residents who stay put.

Keywords: local economic shocks, geographic mobility, consumer financial health, oil boom, credit utilization, delinquency, Consumer Credit Panel.

In brief

Relocating after a local economic bust is associated with stronger household finances: residents who leave oil boom towns after the bust fare better than those who stay.

Using the New York Fed Consumer Credit Panel, the commentary follows residents of areas that saw a surge in oil drilling around 2010 and a bust afterward. Those who relocate after the bust have lower credit utilization, fewer delinquent accounts, and more new credit than those who stay.

Contributes to research on geographic mobility and local economic shocks and household financial health.
