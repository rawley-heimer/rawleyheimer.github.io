Research › Local banking and access to finance

Journal of Financial Economics 134 (3): 591–616

Growing Up Without Finance

James R. Brown, J. Anthony Cookson, and Rawley Z. Heimer

Financial Times list of business school research with social impact. TIAA Paul A. Samuelson Award Finalist.

Abstract. Early-life exposure to local financial institutions increases household financial inclusion and leads to long-term improvements in consumer credit outcomes. We identify the effect of local financial markets using Congressional legislation that led to unintended differences in financial market development across Native American reservations. Individuals from financially underdeveloped reservations enter consumer credit markets later, and upon reaching adulthood, have 10 point lower credit scores and 4 percentage point more delinquent accounts. These effects are long-lived and depreciate slowly after individuals move to more developed areas. Formative exposures to local banking improve consumer credit behavior by increasing financial literacy and financial trust.

Keywords: local banking, bank branches, banking deserts, access to finance, financial inclusion, financial development, credit scores, financial literacy, trust in banks, Native American reservations, early-life exposure, consumer credit.

JEL codes: G21, K40, P48.

In brief

Growing up near banks and other financial institutions has lasting benefits for people’s financial lives.

Congressional legislation created unintended differences in financial development across Native American reservations, which the paper uses to isolate the effect of local financial markets. People from financially underdeveloped reservations enter credit markets later and, as adults, have credit scores about 10 points lower and 4 percentage points more delinquent accounts; these effects fade only slowly even after they move to more developed areas. Early exposure to local banking appears to work by building financial literacy and trust in financial institutions.

Contributes to research on local banking and access to finance, financial inclusion, early-life experiences and household finance, and consumer credit outcomes.
