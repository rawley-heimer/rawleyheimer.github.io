Federal Reserve Bank of Cleveland Working Paper 15-22

Facebook Finance: How Social Interaction Propagates Active Investing

Rawley Z. Heimer and David Simon

Abstract. This paper shows how active investing strategies propagate through social connections in a network of retail traders, using a new database of social activity linked to individual-level trading records. A trader’s good short-term performance causes them to contact others. A trader’s activity increases when peers perform well and increase communication. We use the staggered entry of brokerages into partnerships with the social networking platform, which is a necessary precursor for traders to access the network, to argue these effects are causal. This pattern of communication supports active trading, even though the network reveals the low success rate of retail traders.

Keywords: social transmission bias, retail investors, retail trading, social networks, active trading, retail traders, peer effects, social transmission, investor communication, myForexBook, social trading platform, social media, online investor networks, retail forex trading.

In brief

Social interaction spreads active investing because investors share their successes, not their failures.

Using a social network for traders linked to individual trading records, the paper finds that traders who do well in the short run reach out to others, and that traders trade more when their peers perform well and communicate more. The staggered entry of brokerages into partnerships with the network supports a causal interpretation. This communication pattern promotes active trading even though the network itself reveals how rarely retail traders succeed.

Contributes to research on social transmission bias, peer effects in investing, active trading, and investor social media.
