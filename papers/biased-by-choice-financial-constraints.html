Review of Financial Studies 35 (4): 1643–1681

Biased by Choice: How Financial Constraints Can Reduce Financial Mistakes

Rawley Z. Heimer and Alex Imas

RFS Rising Scholar Award.

Abstract. We show that constraints can improve financial decision-making by disciplining behavioral biases. In financial markets, restrictions on leverage limit traders’ ability to borrow to open new positions. We demonstrate that regulation that restricts the provision of leverage to retail traders improves trading performance. By increasing the opportunity cost of postponing the realization of losses, leverage constraints improve traders’ market timing and reduce their disposition effect. We replicate these findings in two distinct experimental settings, further isolating the mechanism and demonstrating generality of the results. The interaction between constraints and behavioral biases has implications for policy and choice architecture.

Keywords: leverage constraints, disposition effect, behavioral biases, retail traders, financial regulation, choice architecture, experiments.

JEL codes: D18, D91, G11, G40, G41, G50.

In brief

Limits on borrowing can make investors better off by curbing their behavioral biases.

Studying a regulation that restricted how much leverage retail traders could use, the paper finds that constrained traders had better market timing and a weaker disposition effect — the tendency to sell winners too soon and hold on to losers. The mechanism is opportunity cost: when borrowing is limited, keeping a losing position open ties up capital that could fund new trades, so putting off a loss becomes more expensive. Two separate experiments reproduce the result, suggesting that well-designed constraints can work as a form of choice architecture.
